What it is

Anatomy OS is the complete build: infrastructure and skills built for your business, plus enablement and training for your go-to-market (GTM) team to drive adoption. It starts with a two-week sprint that gets your leadership team aligned on the foundations, and ends with those foundations written into a system your team runs every day.

Anatomy OS is what we call it. What you are buying is an AI-native go-to-market operating system, tuned to your business, your brand, and your goals. It starts with the GTM team, because that is where the pressure is, and it is architected to scale across the rest of the organization, so every function that represents the brand is eventually working from the same context.

How it works
Intake, a two-week sprint, then enablement
Before the sprint

Intake

A 40-question intake, which we use to validate your market position, your messaging, and your customers' challenges before anyone is in the room.

Two weeks

Alignment sprint

We meet for one hour a day to verify what the intake found and align your leadership team on each foundation, one at a time.

As we go

Written into the system

Each deliverable is written for internal use, then committed to your repository, where it becomes part of your intelligence layer.

After the sprint

Enable and hand over

Two training and enablement sessions and two hours of office hours, then the documentation, quick guides, and the operating system itself.

In the room

What we align your leadership team on.

Each daily session takes one foundation, tests it against what the intake surfaced, and leaves with a decision rather than a draft.

  • Market position and competition

    Where you sit, who you are really up against, and where the market is moving.

  • Ideal customer profile (ICP)

    Who you sell to, who you do not, and the signals that tell the two apart.

  • Personas

    For each persona: the outcomes they want, the challenges in the way, and the key metrics they are held to.

  • Messaging and positioning

    What you say, how you say it, and the differentiators every skill and agent will carry.

  • Product portfolio

    How the offering fits together, and how each part is positioned for the buyer.

And the other foundations your intake surfaces. The list follows your business, not a template.

Before you book

Questions we get.

  • What happens before the sprint starts?

    You complete a 40-question intake. We use your answers to validate your market position, your messaging, and your customers' challenges, so the sprint sessions are spent confirming and deciding rather than starting from a blank page.

  • How much of our time does it take?

    The intake, then one hour a day with your leadership team across the two-week sprint, then two training sessions and two hours of office hours after it.

  • Who needs to be in the room?

    Your leadership team. The sprint exists to align them, so the decisions made in the hour are the ones that stick.

  • The sprint is two weeks. Why three to six?

    The sprint is two weeks. The rest is the intake before it and the enablement after it, and where a build lands in that range depends on how quickly your team turns around the intake and how fast we can schedule the sessions together.

  • What do we have at the end?

    The documentation, the quick guides, and the operating system itself, with your foundations already written into the repository. Handover follows enablement, so your team is trained on it before it is yours.

Why you need it

A lean go-to-market team is expected to produce like a large one. That is only possible if the work you do once is used every time, by everyone, without being re-explained.

That is what an operating system is. The alternative is what most teams have right now: the same models everyone else has, pointed at context nobody has agreed on, producing more of what the market already sounds like, faster. Humans and AI working as one system is bigger than the sum of its parts. Humans and AI working from different versions of the story is just louder.

How we work

What we hold ourselves to.

  • Built from your own material, never a template, so the differentiation is structural rather than editorial.
  • Standards and governance are written down, so security and finance have something to review rather than a demo to trust.
  • Everything transfers, once your team is enabled on it. There is no version of this where the system leaves when we do.
